Evidence-Based Protection

Understanding Core vs. Lifestyle Vaccines

Veterinary medicine categorizes vaccines into two primary groups: Core vaccines, which are vital for all companion animals regardless of environment, and Non-Core (Lifestyle) vaccines, recommended based on geographic risk, boarding frequency, and outdoor exposure.

Canine Protocols:
  • Core: Rabies (Mandatory), DHPP (Distemper, Hepatitis, Parvovirus, Parainfluenza).
  • Lifestyle: Bordetella (Kennel Cough), Leptospirosis, Lyme Disease, Canine Influenza.
Feline Protocols:
  • Core: Rabies, FVRCP (Feline Viral Rhinotracheitis, Calicivirus, Panleukopenia).
  • Lifestyle: FeLV (Feline Leukemia Virus for outdoor or multi-cat homes).
Comprehensive Protocol

What is Included in Every Vaccination Visit

We never simply inject a vaccine. Every immunization appointment includes a rigorous safety screening to ensure your pet is healthy enough to build antibodies.

Pre-Vaccine Health Exam

Checking body temperature, auscultating heart/lungs, and evaluating lymph nodes. Sick or febrile pets are never vaccinated.

Lifestyle Risk Assessment

Consulting with you regarding dog park visits, hiking habits, or boarding plans to prevent over-vaccination.

Digital Certificate & Passport

Official Rabies certificate and batch number logging instantly updated in your Pet Parent Portal account.

Fear-Free Process

Our Gentle Immunization Process

We turn vaccination visits into positive experiences with gentle handling, distraction treats, and ultra-fine needles.

1. Acclimatization & Treat Distraction

Your pet explores the quiet exam room while receiving pet-safe peanut butter or creamy feline treats.

2. Low-Stress Subcutaneous Injection

Using the finest gauge veterinary needles, vaccines are administered quickly and virtually painlessly.

3. 10-Minute Clinical Monitoring

We observe your companion for any acute hypersensitivity or anaphylactic symptoms before departure.

4. Booster Schedule Auto-Sync

Automated SMS reminders and dashboard passport updates are scheduled for the next booster date.

Post-Vaccine Aftercare Guide

Mild sleepiness or slight tenderness at the injection site for 24 hours is completely normal as the immune system activates.

Normal Behaviors:
Extra napping, mild decrease in appetite for 1 meal, mild soreness.
Call Emergency If:
Facial swelling, hives, repeated vomiting, or difficulty breathing (Extremely rare: <0.05% of patients).
Frequently Asked Questions

Pet Vaccination FAQs

Answers to common questions about vaccination schedules, core requirements, and pet safety.

After the initial puppy/kitten series and 1-year booster, core vaccines such as Rabies and DHPP/FVRCP are typically given every 3 years according to AAHA guidelines. Lifestyle vaccines (such as Bordetella and Leptospirosis) require annual boosters to maintain active antibody protection.

Even strictly indoor cats require the Rabies vaccine (mandated by law) and FVRCP. Viruses such as Panleukopenia are resilient and can be tracked into your home on shoes or clothing. Indoor cats can also escape or encounter stray wildlife such as bats that enter attics.

Yes, VetCare Pro offers vaccine titer testing. A titer test is a simple blood test measuring existing circulating antibody levels against Parvovirus and Distemper. If antibody counts are sufficiently high, revaccination may be postponed safely.
